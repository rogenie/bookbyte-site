If you're self-employed in California, the IRS and the state don't want your estimated tax on the same schedule.

The IRS splits the year into four equal payments. California's Franchise Tax Board asks for 30% in April, 40% in June, nothing in September, and the last 30% in January. Mixing the two up is an easy way to fall short.

We wrote a plain-language 2026 guide. Three takeaways:

1. California's threshold is lower. You generally owe estimated payments if you expect to owe $500 or more to the state, versus $1,000 for the IRS.

2. Last year's tax is the simplest safe harbor. Paying 100% of last year's tax on time (110% if last year's AGI was over $150,000) avoids the federal underpayment penalty, even if you earn more this year.

3. Late beats never. The federal penalty is figured on how much was short and for how many days, so paying late still costs less than not paying.

The next payment for both the IRS and California is due January 15, 2027.
