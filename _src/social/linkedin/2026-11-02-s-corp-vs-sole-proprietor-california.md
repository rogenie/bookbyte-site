Your profit went up, and your self-employment tax went up with it. Should you become an S-corp?

We get this question a lot from California owners. The honest answer: it depends on your numbers, not on what worked for a friend.

Three things to know before you switch:

1. As a sole proprietor, you pay 15.3% self-employment tax on 92.35% of your net profit. As an S-corp owner, Social Security and Medicare tax apply only to the salary you pay yourself.

2. That salary has to be reasonable for the work you do. The IRS can treat a too-low salary's "distributions" as wages.

3. California adds costs: 1.5% of the S-corp's net income with an $800 yearly minimum, plus payroll and an extra business tax return.
