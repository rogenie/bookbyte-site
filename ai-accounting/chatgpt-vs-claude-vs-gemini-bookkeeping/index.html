ChatGPT vs. Claude vs. Gemini for bookkeeping (2026)

You want AI to help with your books. Three names keep coming up, and every review says something different.

By: Computatio · Last reviewed 2026-11-18

Quick answer: as of November 18, 2026, ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all have a free plan and a paid plan near $20 a month (ChatGPT Plus $20, Claude Pro $20, Google AI Pro $19.99). All three can read a spreadsheet or PDF you upload. No review can tell you which one gets your books right. Run the same test file through each one, score the answers against a key, and pick the tool that makes the fewest mistakes you’d miss.

Which AI is best for bookkeeping? Everyone has a different answer

One article says ChatGPT. Another says Claude. A friend swears by Gemini. Most of those reviews test essays and code, not a month of bank transactions.

Bookkeeping is a narrow job, so test the tool on that job.

And you don’t know what happens to your data

Your bank export holds client names, amounts and habits. Each company handles chats differently, and the default settings on personal plans are not the most private ones. You’d rather not find that out later.

What picking the wrong tool costs you

  • Quiet errors. A wrong category doesn’t throw an error. It sits in your books until tax time, when someone sorts it again.
  • Records you still have to back up. The IRS says to keep income tax records for 3 years in most cases, and 6 years if you leave out income worth more than 25% of the gross income on your return. An AI chat is not a record. Your receipts and statements are.
  • Privacy you gave away by default. Google says a subset of Gemini chats are read by human reviewers, and it advises against entering confidential information you wouldn’t want a reviewer to see.

Picture this instead

You spend 20 minutes on one afternoon. You feed the same made-up month to each tool and score the answers. You end up with one tool, set up with the privacy settings you chose, and a test file you can rerun when the tools change.

Set privacy first, before any test

  1. ChatGPT: in Settings, turn off the option that lets OpenAI train on your content. OpenAI also lets you file a “do not train on my content” request in its privacy portal.
  2. Claude: go to Settings > Privacy and switch off Help improve our AI models. Anthropic says this applies to new chats from then on.
  3. Gemini: turn off History. Google says this keeps new chats out of model training unless you send feedback. Reviewed chats can be kept for up to three years.

Then strip the test file anyway. Our guide to keeping financial data safe with AI lists what to remove.

The 20-minute bookkeeping bake-off

Use made-up data, the same file and the same prompt in every tool. Start a new chat each time.

  1. Build a test file. Ten to fifteen rows with Date, Description and Amount. Add three traps: a personal charge, a duplicated row, and a vague payee.
  2. Write your answer key first. Decide the right category for every row before any AI sees it.
  3. Upload the file and paste the prompt below.
  4. Score every row. One point per correct category. One point for each trap the tool flags.
  5. Count the confident mistakes. A wrong answer with no doubt attached is worse than a question.
Prompt to copy:
You are helping a sole proprietor with bookkeeping. For each row in the attached file, give a category from this list: Income, Advertising, Contract labor, Insurance, Office expense, Supplies, Meals, Utilities, Software, Personal (not business), Ask owner. Use Ask owner whenever you are not sure. Flag any row that looks duplicated. Return a table with Date, Description, Amount, Category, and a one-line reason. Then give total income and total business expenses, and show your math. Do not invent rows.
Example (made-up data). A one-person photography business, October 2026.
Rows: Client A $1,800 · Client B $950 · editing software $54.99 · memory cards $89.00 · second shooter $400.00 · liability insurance $42.00 · client coffee $18.60 · phone and internet $85.00 · streaming service $15.49 · editing software $54.99 (same date again) · “SQ *JM LLC” $120.00.
Answer key: two Income rows; Software, Supplies, Contract labor, Insurance, Meals, Utilities; streaming is Personal; the second software row is a duplicate; “SQ *JM LLC” is Ask owner. Income $2,750.00. Business expenses $689.59 once the duplicate and the personal charge are left out, before you sort out the $120 payee.
Scoring one sample run: 9 of 11 categories right, the duplicate caught, the streaming charge called Software. That last one is a confident mistake, and it’s the kind you’d miss at a glance.
These rows and scores are invented to show the method. They are not results from any of the three tools.

How to check the winner’s work every month

  • Tie the totals. AI income and expense totals must match the sum of the rows in your file, to the cent.
  • Map categories to Schedule C. Sole proprietors report expenses by line: contract labor (line 11), insurance other than health (line 15), office expense (line 18), supplies (line 22), deductible meals (line 24b) and utilities (line 25). Check that each category lands on one line.
  • Record meals in full. The IRS says most business meals are only 50% deductible, but the $18.60 coffee stays $18.60 in the books. The limit is applied on the return.
  • Reconcile. No chat tool replaces matching your books to the bank statement.

Is ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini accurate enough for my books?

Accurate enough for a first pass, if you check the output. All three can misread a vague payee or a personal charge. The test shows you where your pick slips, so you know what to look at. For a fuller list of strengths and limits, see Can AI do your accounting?
