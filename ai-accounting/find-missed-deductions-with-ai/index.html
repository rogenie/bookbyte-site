How to use AI to find missed deductions, and verify them

You paid for software, mileage and a home office all year. You’re not sure all of it made it onto your return.

By: Computatio · Last reviewed 2026-11-26

Quick answer. To find missed deductions with AI, export a year of bank and card transactions, ask the AI to list business-looking charges you haven’t categorized, and compare them to your books. Then verify each one: it must be a business cost, you need a receipt, and mixed-use items must be split. AI finds candidates. You decide what you can claim.

You think you’re missing deductions

Business costs hide in personal accounts. A software plan on your personal card. Parking at a client meeting. A phone you use for both.

A cost you never recorded can’t reduce your tax.

And you’re afraid to claim the wrong ones

The internet is full of “write off everything” advice. You don’t want to claim groceries and hope. So you leave real costs out instead.

What missed deductions cost you

  • More self-employment tax. Self-employment tax is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare) once your net earnings reach $400. Every real expense you skip leaves your net earnings, and that tax, higher.
  • Lost money from past years. To claim a refund on an amended return, the IRS says you must file within 3 years after you filed the original return or 2 years after you paid the tax, whichever is later.
  • Risk the other way. The IRS says personal, living or family expenses are generally not deductible. Claiming them because a tool suggested it is still your mistake.

Picture this instead

It’s January. You have a short list of extra business costs you found in your own data. Each one has a receipt, a business reason and a category. You hand your preparer facts, not guesses.

The find-and-verify method for missed deductions with AI

This works in any AI chat tool. Before you paste anything, remove account numbers and anything you don’t need. Our guide to keeping financial data safe with AI lists what to strip.

  1. Export a full year. Download 12 months of transactions from every account you might have used for business, including personal cards. Keep date, payee and amount.
  2. Export your books. Pull the same year’s categorized expenses from your bookkeeping tool or spreadsheet.
  3. Ask the AI to find candidates. Use the first prompt below. It compares the two lists and flags business-looking charges missing from your books.
  4. Ask the AI to check common gaps. Use the second prompt. It asks you questions instead of assuming.
  5. Verify every candidate. Run the checks in the next section before anything goes in your books.
  6. Record what passes. Add each verified expense to your books with its category and a note. Our prompts to categorize transactions help here.
Prompt 1: find candidates.
I run a [type of business]. List A is every transaction from my bank and card accounts for [year] (date, payee, amount). List B is the expenses already in my books. Find charges in List A that are not in List B and could be business costs for my kind of business. For each one, give the date, payee, amount, a possible category, and one question I must answer to confirm it’s business. Don’t decide for me. Mark anything that looks personal as “Likely personal.”
Prompt 2: check common gaps.
Ask me yes-or-no questions, one at a time, about costs self-employed people often forget: business driving, a home office, software and subscriptions, phone and internet, bank and payment fees, supplies, and professional fees. After each “yes,” tell me what record I need to support it. Don’t estimate amounts.
Example (made-up data). A freelance designer runs Prompt 1 on 2026 data.
AI’s candidates: a design-font subscription, $180 on a personal card; parking near a client office, $42; a streaming service, $96; a laptop stand, $65.
After checking: the font plan is used only for client work and has an emailed receipt, so it goes in the books. Parking matches a client meeting on her calendar. The laptop stand has a receipt and sits on her work desk.
What she rejected: the streaming service. The AI called it “research.” She watches it for fun, so it’s personal.

How to verify a deduction AI found

Run each candidate through these checks. If it fails one, leave it out.

  1. Is it a business cost? It must be a cost of running your business. The IRS says personal, living or family expenses generally aren’t deductible.
  2. Do you have proof? Find the receipt or invoice and the bank or card line. No proof, no claim.
  3. Is it mixed use? The IRS says when an expense like your vehicle is for both business and personal use, you must divide it between the two. Claim only the business part.
  4. Did you already claim it? Search your books for the same amount and date. AI lists often repeat a charge that was recorded under a different name.
  5. Check the math yourself. Add totals with a SUM formula. Never copy an AI total onto a tax form.

AI finds candidates. You confirm every one. AI doesn’t replace receipts or reconciling, and you stay responsible for your return.

Which deductions do self-employed people miss most?

These are common places to look. Each has its own rules, so check them before you claim.

  • Business driving. For 2026, the IRS business standard mileage rate is 72.5 cents per mile. You need a log of business trips. See our AI mileage log guide.
  • Home office. The space must be used regularly and exclusively for business, and generally be your principal place of business. The IRS simplified method is $5 per square foot of the business space, up to 300 square feet. The regular method uses Form 8829.
  • Half of self-employment tax. When figuring adjusted gross income, you can deduct one-half of your self-employment tax. Tax software usually figures it, but check it’s there.
  • Costs on the wrong card. Business costs paid from a personal account are easy to miss. Our guide to separating business and personal expenses helps stop this next year.
State note. Your state return may not follow every federal rule. Before claiming a deduction on a state return, including in California, check your state tax agency’s instructions.

What if you find deductions from a past year?

If you missed a real deduction on a return you already filed, the fix is an amended return, Form 1040-X. Watch the refund window above. Bring your verified list and receipts to whoever prepares your return. Our guide to preparing for your tax appointment with AI shows how to package it.
