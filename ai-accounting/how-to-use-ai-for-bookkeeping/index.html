AI for Accounting

How to use AI for bookkeeping, and keep your books accurate

AI can do the slowest parts of bookkeeping in minutes: sorting transactions, reading receipts, drafting summaries. It also makes confident mistakes. Here’s how to get the speed without the errors.

By: Computatio · Last reviewed 2026-10-21

New to the topic? Start with our AI for Accounting guides, or see the bookkeeping basics first.

What AI is good at

  • Categorizing transactions from the bank description, once you give it your category list and a few rules.
  • Reading receipts and invoices and pulling out the date, vendor, amount and what was bought.
  • Spotting oddities such as duplicate charges, a subscription that doubled, or a payment with no matching invoice.
  • Explaining your reports in plain English, such as why profit dropped this month.
  • Drafting routine writing like invoice reminders and notes for your tax preparer.

What AI gets wrong

  • It can’t know your context. A charge at a warehouse store might be office supplies or groceries. Only you know.
  • It states tax rules confidently, even when they’re outdated or wrong. Rates, thresholds and deadlines change every year. Check any rule against an IRS or FTB page.
  • It can slip on arithmetic across long tables. Totals should come from your bookkeeping software or spreadsheet, not from a chat answer.
  • It doesn’t reconcile. Matching your books to your bank statement is still your job, or your bookkeeper’s.

A safe workflow for categorizing with AI

  1. Export last month’s transactions from your bank or bookkeeping software as a spreadsheet file.
  2. Remove sensitive details such as full account numbers before you paste or upload anything.
  3. Give it your categories. Use the expense lines from Schedule C plus “owner draw” and “owner contribution.” Our bookkeeping guide explains why.
  4. Ask for a reason and a confidence level for each category, using the prompt below.
  5. Review every low-confidence item and anything over an amount you choose, such as $200.
  6. Reconcile the month against your bank statement in your bookkeeping system. If the totals don’t match, the categories don’t matter yet.
  7. Keep the receipts. AI summaries don’t replace the records the IRS expects you to keep.
Prompt you can copy:
“You are helping me categorize transactions for a self-employed business. Use only these categories: [paste your list]. Rules: [for example, ‘Shell, Chevron = car and truck expenses’; ‘transfers to savings = owner draw’]. For each transaction, return the date, description, amount, category, a confidence level (high, medium or low) and a one-line reason. Mark anything that could be personal as low confidence. Don’t total anything.”
Example (made-up data). Three lines a run of the prompt might return:
Oct 3 · ADOBE *CREATIVE CLD · $59.99 → Office expense · high · “Recurring software subscription.”
Oct 9 · COSTCO WHSE #0412 · $214.37 → Supplies · low · “Warehouse store; could be personal groceries.”
Oct 15 · TRANSFER TO SAV 4471 · $1,500.00 → Owner draw · medium · “Transfer to savings; could be a tax set-aside.”
How to check it: open the Costco receipt before accepting the category, confirm the transfer went to your own account, and make sure every line appears exactly once compared with your bank export.

Keep your data safe

  • Never paste Social Security numbers, full bank or card numbers, or passwords into an AI chat.
  • Check the tool’s data settings, including whether your inputs can be used to train its models, and turn that off where you can.
  • If you handle other people’s financial information, such as clients or employees, use a business plan with a clear data agreement, or leave that data out.

You’re still responsible for the result

The IRS holds taxpayers responsible for what’s on their return, whoever, or whatever, prepared it. Treat AI like a fast assistant whose work you check, not a replacement for checking.

A realistic monthly routine with AI

  • Weekly: snap receipts and let AI extract the details.
  • Month-end: run the categorizing prompt, review the flagged items, then reconcile.
  • Quarterly: ask AI to summarize the quarter’s profit, then check the numbers against your profit and loss report before you make estimated tax payments.
