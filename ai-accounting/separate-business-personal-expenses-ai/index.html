AI for Accounting

Separate business and personal expenses with AI

Your business card paid for groceries. Your personal card paid for a software plan. Now tax time is coming, and you have to work out which charges belong to the business.

By: Computatio · Last reviewed 2026-11-04

Quick answer: To separate business and personal expenses with AI, export your transactions, give the AI your category list and a short set of your own rules, and ask it to mark every line business, personal or split, with a reason and no totals. Then you decide every split line, keep the receipt, and post the results in your books. The IRS says personal, living and family costs generally aren’t deductible, and costs used both ways, like a car, must be divided.

Your business and personal spending are mixed together

It started small. One coffee on the wrong card. One client lunch on your personal card. A few months later, nobody can tell which charge was which, including you.

Mixed accounts turn a 10-minute monthly job into a weekend of guessing.

And some expenses are both at once

Your phone, your car, your internet and the room you work in all serve your life and your business. You know part of each is deductible. You don’t know how much, or how to show it.

What mixed expenses cost you

  • Personal costs don’t count. The IRS says personal, household and family living costs generally can’t be deducted, because they aren’t ordinary and necessary costs of your business. Leave one in, and your profit is too low on your return.
  • Mixed-use costs must be split. The IRS says you must divide them between business and personal use. For a vehicle, you deduct only the business part. Deduct the whole thing and you overstate the expense.
  • Some rules are specific. For 2025, the Schedule C instructions say you can’t deduct the base rate of the first phone line into your home. A home office has to be used regularly and exclusively for business. Business meals are generally limited to 50%.
  • A bank line isn’t proof. IRS Publication 583 says proof of payment alone doesn’t show you’re entitled to a deduction. You need the receipt or invoice too.

Picture a month where every charge has a home

You open last month’s transactions. AI has already marked 90% of them business or personal, with a reason for each. Six lines say “split.” You decide those six with your receipts and mileage log, and you’re done.

You make the judgment calls. AI does the sorting.

The business-or-personal method, step by step

These steps work in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot. You don’t need to be technical. You need a transaction export and 30 minutes.

  1. Export one month of transactions from each account the business touched, business and personal. Keep only date, description and amount. Remove account numbers, names and anything else personal; see our data safety guide.
  2. Write your house rules in three to five lines. For example: “Groceries are personal. Adobe and Zoom are business. Phone and car are split.” AI can’t know your business. Your rules tell it.
  3. Run the prompt below. It sorts, explains and flags. It doesn’t total anything.
  4. Decide every “split” and “unsure” line yourself. Use a reasonable basis you can show, like a mileage log for your car or a log of business calls for your phone.
  5. Post the results in your books. Business costs paid from a personal account are usually recorded as an owner contribution. Personal costs paid from the business account are owner draws, not expenses.
Prompt you can copy:
“Help me separate business and personal spending for my [type of business]. My categories are: [paste list]. My rules: [paste your 3–5 rules]. For each transaction, return date, description, amount, a label (business, personal, split or unsure), a suggested category if business, and a one-line reason. Use ‘split’ for anything that may be used both ways, like a phone, car, internet or home. Use ‘unsure’ when you’d need a receipt to decide. Don’t guess a business percentage and don’t calculate totals. Transactions: [paste].”

Two lines in that prompt do the heavy lifting: no guessed percentages, and no totals. The percentage needs your records. The totals come from your software.

Example (made-up data). A freelance photographer runs one month of 62 transactions from her business and personal cards:
Oct 3 · TRADER JOE’S · $74.18 → personal · “Groceries, per your rules.”
Oct 6 · ADOBE CREATIVE CLOUD · $59.99 → business · Software · “Listed as business in your rules.” Paid from her personal card, so she records it as an owner contribution.
Oct 9 · VERIZON WIRELESS · $95.00 → split · “Phone used both ways; needs your business share.”
Oct 14 · SHELL OIL · $48.20 → split · “Car fuel; depends on your mileage log.”
Oct 22 · BLUE PLATE CAFE · $63.50 → unsure · “Could be a client meal or personal; check the receipt.”
Result: 55 lines sorted, 5 split, 2 unsure. She checks the 7 open lines against her receipts and mileage log, then posts the month.

How do you check AI’s business vs. personal split?

  1. Count the rows. Every transaction you pasted should come back once.
  2. Spot-check five “business” lines against receipts. Each record should show the payee, amount, date, proof of payment and what it was for.
  3. Never accept a percentage AI made up. Your car share comes from your mileage log. Your phone and home office shares come from your own records.
  4. Reconcile each account in your software. See AI bank reconciliation. If it doesn’t tie out, the month isn’t done.

Stop the mixing at the source

AI helps you clean up. It doesn’t stop the mess from coming back. Publication 583 says to keep your business account separate from your personal checking account. One business account and one business card cut the split work to a handful of lines a month.

Is this safe, and can AI get it wrong?

AI can get it wrong, which is why it labels and you decide. Never paste Social Security numbers, full account numbers or passwords. And remember that you’re responsible for what goes on your return, whoever or whatever sorted it.
