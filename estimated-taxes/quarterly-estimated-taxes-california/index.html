Estimated taxes

Quarterly estimated taxes in California: a 2026 guide

You’re self-employed in California. Nobody withholds tax from your income, so you’re supposed to pay it yourself during the year. But how much? When? And to whom: the IRS, the state, or both?

By: Computatio · Last reviewed 2026-10-23

Quick answer: if you expect to owe at least $1,000 to the IRS or $500 to California for 2026, you generally must pay estimated tax. The IRS takes four equal payments (April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15, 2027). California takes 30% in April, 40% in June, nothing in September and 30% in January. Paying 100% of last year’s tax on time (110% if last year’s AGI was over $150,000) avoids the underpayment penalty.

Two tax agencies, two different schedules

Here’s the first problem. The IRS and California’s Franchise Tax Board (FTB) both want estimated payments, but not on the same plan.

PaymentIRSCalifornia (FTB)
1April 15, 2026: 25%April 15, 2026: 30%
2June 15, 2026: 25%June 15, 2026: 40%
3September 15, 2026: 25%September 15, 2026: 0%
4January 15, 2027: 25%January 15, 2027: 30%

California wants 70% of the year’s estimate by June 15, and nothing in September. If you pay the state on the IRS schedule, you fall behind by June.

And you don’t know how much to pay

The second problem: your income moves. A big client in March, a slow summer. You can’t know this year’s tax until the year is over, so any number feels like a guess.

The federal amount isn’t just income tax, either. It also covers self-employment tax: 15.3% (12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of your net earnings. The Social Security part applies only up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500.

What it costs to guess wrong

  • The penalty grows every day. The federal underpayment penalty is figured separately for each due date, on the amount that was short and the number of days it stayed unpaid.
  • Catching up late doesn’t erase it. A later payment is applied first to the earliest unpaid installment, so a short April payment keeps costing you until it’s covered.
  • The cash isn’t there. Guess low all year and you face one large bill in April, on top of the first payment for the new year.

Picture this instead

Four dates on your calendar. A tax savings account that already holds the money when each one arrives. Payments sized by a rule, not a guess. No underpayment penalty, even in a year your income jumps. That’s what the method below sets up.

How much estimated tax to pay in California: the last-year method

The safe harbor rules give you a way to pay without predicting the future. You avoid the underpayment penalty if your payments (plus any withholding) cover the smaller of:

  • 90% of this year’s tax, or
  • 100% of last year’s tax, or 110% if last year’s adjusted gross income was over $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separately).

California uses the same 90% / 100% / 110% tests, with one addition: if your California adjusted gross income this year is $1,000,000 or more ($500,000 if married or RDP filing separately), you must base payments on 90% of this year’s tax.

California’s threshold is lower: you generally must pay if you expect to owe at least $500 ($250 if married or RDP filing separately). For the IRS it’s $1,000.

Step by step

  1. Find last year’s total tax on your 2025 federal and California returns.
  2. Multiply by 100%, or 110% if your 2025 AGI was over $150,000.
  3. Split it: four equal payments for the IRS; 30/40/0/30 for California.
  4. Put all four dates on your calendar now.
  5. Open a separate savings account for taxes and move a fixed share of every client payment into it. Pick the share from last year’s total tax divided by last year’s income.
  6. Once a month, update and reconcile your books so you can see if this year is running far above last year.
  7. Pay on time. IRS Direct Pay or EFTPS for the IRS; FTB Web Pay for California, which lets you schedule a payment up to a year ahead. Paper payments use Form 540-ES vouchers. Keep every confirmation number.
Example (made-up data). Ana is a freelance designer in San Diego. Her 2025 AGI was under $150,000, and her 2025 returns showed $12,000 total federal tax and $3,000 California tax.
Federal: 100% of $12,000 = four payments of $3,000.
California: 100% of $3,000 on the 30/40/0/30 schedule = $900 on April 15, $1,200 on June 15, nothing on September 15, $900 on January 15, 2027.
If Ana earns much more in 2026, she’ll owe the difference when she files, but no underpayment penalty.
Next payment: the fourth 2026 payment for both the IRS and California is due January 15, 2027. For the IRS only, you can skip it if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the full balance with it.

What if I miss a California or IRS estimated payment?

Pay as soon as you can. Because the penalty is figured by the day, a late payment usually costs less than no payment. If most of your income came late in the year, the annualized income method on Form 2210 may lower or remove the federal penalty.

What if my estimated payments keep growing?

If your profit keeps climbing, look at how your business is set up. Some owners compare an S corporation with staying a sole proprietor. See S-corp vs. sole proprietor, or check your projection with AI and a line-by-line math check. More in our estimated tax guides.
