Business meal deduction: track meals with AI

You buy a client lunch, pay with your card and move on. In April you stare at a pile of restaurant charges and can’t remember which ones were business.

By: Computatio · Last reviewed 2026-11-27

Quick answer. For 2025 returns, the IRS says you can generally deduct only 50% of business meal costs, including tax and tip. You (or your employee) must be at the meal, it can’t be lavish, and it must be with a business contact such as a client or customer. Entertainment isn’t deductible. AI can sort your restaurant charges and draft the log. You confirm every meal.

Your restaurant charges are a mystery

Coffee with a prospect. A team lunch. A Friday dinner with your family. On a card statement they all look the same.

A meal you can’t explain is a meal you can’t defend.

And the business meal rules feel fuzzy

Is a client golf outing a meal? Is the full bill deductible or half? Old articles still say 100%. That was a temporary rule for restaurant meals paid in 2021 and 2022, and it’s over.

What sloppy meal records cost you

  • The wrong amount. Record a meal at full cost on your return and you overstate the deduction. The 50% limit applies to the meal, the tax and the tip.
  • Entertainment mixed in. The IRS says expenses for entertainment, amusement or recreation are no longer deductible. Food at a game or show counts only if it was bought separately or listed separately on the bill.
  • No proof when asked. The IRS generally wants documentary evidence, such as receipts, for expenses of $75 or more. And it says records made at or near the time carry more weight than notes made later.

Picture this instead

Every restaurant charge in your books has a category, a name and a reason. Personal meals sit in owner draws. Client meals sit in their own “Meals” account. At tax time, your total is one number you trust.

The 3-pass meal log method with AI

This works in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot. Before you paste, remove card numbers and anything you don’t need. Our guide to financial data safety with AI lists what to strip.

  1. Pass 1: pull the restaurant charges. Export a month of bank and card transactions (date, payee, amount). Ask the AI to list only food and drink charges.
  2. Pass 2: sort them. The AI asks you about each one and drafts a log line. It never guesses business purpose.
  3. Pass 3: check and record. Match each line to a receipt and your calendar. Then record it in your books.
Prompt you can copy:
“Below are my business and personal card transactions for [month]. List only charges at restaurants, cafes, bars or food delivery. For each one, ask me: was this business or personal? If business, who was there and what business did we discuss? Was it tied to entertainment, like a game or show? Then build a table: date, payee, amount, who was present, business purpose, receipt yes/no, and a category of Meals, Entertainment (not deductible), or Personal. Don’t fill in a purpose I didn’t give you. Flag any charge of $75 or more without a receipt.”
Example (made-up data). A freelance web designer runs the prompt on October charges.
Oct 3 · CORNER CAFE · $18.40 → Meals · with prospect J. Lee, website quote · receipt yes
Oct 9 · HARBOR GRILL · $142.75 → Meals · with client A. Park, redesign kickoff · flagged: no receipt, $75 or more
Oct 17 · STADIUM CONCESSIONS · $36.00 → Entertainment (not deductible) · snacks at a game with a client, not billed separately from the tickets
Oct 24 · PIZZA PLACE · $54.10 → Personal · family dinner
What she did next: asked Harbor Grill for a copy of the receipt, moved the pizza to owner draws, and kept the stadium charge out of Meals.

How to check the AI’s meal log

  1. Match every line to the statement. Same date, same payee, same amount. AI sometimes merges or repeats charges.
  2. Match the receipt. The IRS says a restaurant receipt should show the restaurant’s name and location, the number of people served, and the date and amount.
  3. Check the purpose came from you. If the AI wrote a reason you never gave, delete it.
  4. Record the full cost. Your books hold what you paid. The 50% limit is applied on your return, not by cutting the expense in half in your books.
  5. Add the total yourself. Use a SUM formula on the Meals account. Don’t copy an AI total onto a tax form.

AI drafts the log. You supply the facts and the proof. It doesn’t replace receipts, reconciling or your responsibility for your return.

Which business meals are deductible?

Per the IRS 2025 guidance, a business meal is generally deductible at 50% when:

  • It’s an ordinary and necessary business cost.
  • You or your employee is present.
  • It isn’t lavish or extravagant (reasonable for the facts).
  • It’s with a current or potential customer, client, consultant or similar business contact.

Meals while traveling away from home overnight for business also fall under the 50% limit. Transportation to and from a meal isn’t part of the meal’s cost.

Are any business meals fully deductible?

The IRS lists exceptions to the 50% limit. A few that come up for small businesses:

SituationRule (2025 IRS guidance)
Holiday party or summer picnic for employeesNot subject to the 50% limit
You sell meals to the publicNot subject to the 50% limit
Meals treated as wages to an employeeNot subject to the 50% limit
Workers under Department of Transportation hours-of-service limits80% instead of 50%

Exceptions have conditions. Read the Pub. 463 section before you rely on one.

Where do meals go on Schedule C?

Sole proprietors put deductible business meals on Schedule C, line 24b, separate from travel on line 24a. Entertainment goes on neither line. Keep a separate Meals account in your books so the limit is easy to apply. Our chart of accounts guide shows how, and our transaction categorizing prompts already include a Meals category.

State note. States don’t always follow federal rules. California, for example, didn’t adopt the temporary federal 100% restaurant meal deduction for 2021 and 2022. Check your state tax agency’s instructions before you file.

Make it a monthly habit

Run the prompt once a month, not once a year. Snap each receipt the same day and note who you met, as our AI receipt scanning guide shows. Fifteen minutes a month beats a weekend of guessing in April.
